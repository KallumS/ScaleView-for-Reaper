## How it started

In Noterator and Miderator the Chords lane disagreed with the Blocks toolbox
over inversions. The user asked for every Blocks chord in every key to be
checked against the lane, with Blocks right wherever they disagree, then -
after reading every doc here first, at their request - for Pro itself to take
Blocks as its dictionary, earlier preferences (Scaler, the C6 convention)
giving way where they conflict.

## What was measured, in order

1. Every Blocks chord - 288 keys, eight families, every type, degree and
   inversion, 670,248 - generated by Blocks itself through Noterator's
   adapter and named by the lane: no name claimed wrong notes, 52% on
   Blocks' root, and 19% sharing exact notes and bass with a Blocks chord on
   another root. That last figure is why a reader alone cannot reach 100%;
   Noterator records the root with an inserted Blocks chord instead (its
   decision 0046) and gets 100% there.
2. Root-choice rules over a Blocks dictionary, scored against those chords:
   majority vote 85.0% (ceiling 85.7%), bass first 83.8%, reader's cost
   among in-key roots 83.4%, Blocks' types on any root 74.4%. Then the same
   rules on 20,027 lead-sheet symbols: in-key 97.93%, any root 99.33%,
   before 99.27%. Put to the user with both columns; they chose any root.
3. The final version: the sweep, Scaler's 24, Wikipedia, jazznet, the lead
   sheets and the whole music21 core corpus (rescanned, 490,732 sonorities,
   no failures) - see decision 0011 for the figures.

## What went wrong, and what it cost

- **A Lua evaluation-order bug** - `voiced[#voiced + 1] = table.remove(voiced,
  1) + 12` counts the slot before the removal - put holes in every inverted
  voicing, and C E G read `D11/C`. Found by dumping one dictionary entry and
  tracing which shapes landed on it, not by staring at the code.
- **Majority voting looked best on the numbers** and read a plain Csus4 as
  `Fsus2/C`. The score being maximised was Blocks' menu, which offers some
  notes more ways than others; the duplicate "3rd inversion" of a triad made
  that worse (Eaug/C for C E G#). The user's own note that a triad has no
  third inversion and a ninth chord has a fourth settled how inversions are
  counted.
- **The in-key rule looked faithful and read real music worse** - a Bm7 in F
  major as D6/B. Only the lead sheets showed it, which is why the decision
  went to the user with both measurements.
- `git stash` was used once to compare before and after, in a repository
  with nothing else uncommitted, and popped straight back.

## Later: Blocks' inversions, and does the dictionary change?

Starting Blocks now offers each chord the inversions it has (its 0007): a
triad two, a seventh three, up to six for a thirteenth, where it offered
Root to 3rd on everything. Asked whether the dictionary had to follow: no.
It reads a chord by its notes with any of them in the bass, and neither
`BLOCKS_CHORDS` nor `BLOCKS_DIATONIC` changed. Measured to be sure, over
every Blocks chord in 288 keys and every inversion it has (735,822, through
Noterator's byte-identical copy of this reader, with no recorded root):
71.2% named on Blocks' root over its bass - 97.0% in root position, 60-70%
for the 1st to 5th inversions, 30.2% for a thirteenth's 6th, which is vi13
in root position note for note. The 74.4% above was measured on the old
Root-to-3rd menu and is not comparable.
