Both Lua suites pass. Neither script changed.

## How it started

The user uploaded the repository as it stood after the Scaler Detector runs
(1 and 6-7 November: 231 chords of 2-4 notes, 1,254 of 5-7) and asked whether
the reader could be refined - without a chord list - to agree with Scaler more
often, ignoring the `?` readings.

## What was done

- The upload's uncommitted work came in first: the 5-7 note data, the
  vocabulary file, the corrected `scaler_cases.py` and CLAUDE.md's write-up.
- `tools/runner.lua` and `tools/chord_types.lua` had been broken since 1.2.0:
  the scripts call `gfx.getfont()` from `pickFace()` and the tools' mocks had
  no such function, so every measuring tool that drives the script crashed on
  load. Both now answer as an OS that only has Arial would.
- `tools/scaler_agree.py` scores any build against all 803 named chords. It
  reproduced the recorded agreement figures exactly before it was used.
- Weights, five structural rules and four loosenings of decision 0011 were
  each measured against Scaler and against every other reference. The table
  is in CLAUDE.md, *Can the reader agree with Scaler more often?*

## What was learned

- Weights buy at most 6 of 803. Rules with a musical case buy 3 to 7 and each
  costs a human-written reference or renames hundreds of sweep voicings.
- Decision 0011 overrides the reader's own (Scaler-agreeing) choice 47 times,
  sometimes badly (`Amin11/C` for Cmaj13 without its 11th), but every
  loosening tried brings back faults it was repairing. Left for the user.

## What went wrong

- A claim written into CLAUDE.md from memory - that the free reader turns
  Cmaj7 over G into an odd chord on G - was false; it reads `Cmaj7/G`. Caught
  by running it before committing, and replaced with two cases that were
  checked (Dbmaj9 over Ab, C7b9 over G).
- Lua treats an empty environment variable as set, so the first "baseline" of
  one experiment was secretly the variant. Spotted because two rows matched.
