## The short answer to "is it 100% accurate"

**Yes, on the question that has a right answer, and it was already.** Every
name Pro prints describes exactly the notes played, with the right bass: 100%
of 20,027 lead-sheet chords, 100% of the 17,688-voicing sweep in every key
tried, and the corpus figures in `CLAUDE.md` stand.

**No, and it never can be, on the other question** - does it print the *same
name* a human wrote. Two musicians do not agree on that: `Fm7/Bb` and `Bb11`
are the same five notes, and so are `C6` and `Am7/C`. Measured anyway:

| against | names the human's root |
| --- | --- |
| 502 public-domain lead sheets (OpenEWLD), 20,027 symbols | **99.27%** |
| Beethoven's 32 first movements, analyst's Roman numerals (BPS-FH) | **94.8%**, 98.5% excluding diminished sevenths and other sets with no derivable root |

Every remaining difference on the lead sheets is one of: a triad written over
a foreign bass, an inversion in the C6/Am7 family, an arranger's odd spelling
of a plain chord (`Am#5` for F/A), or a symmetric chord with no derivable
root. None is a misnamed chord. The nearest thing to a complaint is `E9/B`
reading `Bmin6Add11`, three times - see *Considered and left alone*. That breakdown is in `CLAUDE.md`, *Against the symbols people
write*.

## What the review found

### A wrong name the 100% figures could not see

The lead-sheet round trip was new, and on its first page it printed `E7#5b9`
coming back as `Dmin9b5/E`. Probed directly, all the standard altered
dominants with an altered fifth were wrong the same way - `C7#5b9` as
`A#min9b5/C`, `C7b5#9` as `D#min6b9/C`. Every one of those names accounts for
exactly the notes, which is why a million sonorities at 100% never showed it.

The cause was one clause: any alteration over an altered fifth was charged as
a sign the root was wrong. Over a dominant on its own root it is the chord.
The first fix allowed it from any bass and moved 790 sweep names, broke
`Cmaj7#5#11`, and produced `Aaug7#9/C` - a #9 in the bass. Restricting it to
root position, which is how the chord is played, moved 455 and only the
intended shapes among five-note chords.

→ decision [0009](../decisions/0009-an-altered-dominant-keeps-its-root.md)

### A tiebreak that chose a slash

C D G Bb over C cost the same as `C7sus2` and as `GminAdd11/C`, and the minor
triad's rank won. A draw now goes to the reading without a slash, after the
key and before the commoner quality. 132 sweep names move.

On real music it matters more than the altered-dominant fix: 0.81% of the
music21 core corpus (2,955 of 363,963 sonorities) is renamed, almost all
quartal and suspended voicings - G D F A from `DminAdd11/G` to `G7sus2`. The
altered-dominant rule renames 11. Neither moves the objective score:
363,959 of 363,963, the same four eight-note clusters as before.

→ decision [0010](../decisions/0010-a-draw-goes-to-the-reading-without-a-slash.md)

### MIDI events silently dropped

The poll read at most 64 events and discarded anything older. Pitch bend and
pressure stream while keys are held, and the defer loop stops while a menu is
open, so a note-off could be pushed past the cap and left ringed. The test
puts a note-off behind 600 other messages; it failed on the old code. The walk
already stopped at the last event applied, so the cap is now only a safety
stop.

### Checked and found sound

- **Simple really is Pro minus the chord reader.** Diffed: apart from its
  name, settings key and label, every line in it is a line of Pro.
- The menu settle, the dock bitfield, settings by name, project state and the
  redraw-on-held-set fix all read correctly and are covered by the suites.

### Considered and left alone

- **`E9/B` reads `Bmin6Add11`, `E7b5/B` reads `BminMaj7(11,13)`.** Root
  position winning over a plainer chord in inversion is `COST_INVERSION`, and
  `CLAUDE.md` records that moving it costs 12% of the sweep for no gain
  against Scaler. A preference, not a defect - raise it with the user if it
  bothers them.
- **Sustain pedal.** Pro shows the keys held, not the notes sounding under the
  pedal. A design choice; a CC64 option would be a feature, not a fix.
- **Note-offs ignore the MIDI channel.** The same note held on two channels and
  released on one clears both. Rare outside MPE; not changed.

## What went wrong, and what it cost

- **The classifier in my own lead-sheet tool was wrong first time.** It asked
  whether the slash bass was in the chord *kind*, before the file's degree
  alterations were applied, so `E7b5/B` counted as an inversion. Found by
  reading the misses rather than the totals; fixed before any figure was
  quoted.
- **`CHORD_TYPES.md` said 496 distinct chord types; the shipped engine printed
  500.** Stale already, and nobody would have known without regenerating it.
  The sweep is committed now, which is the actual fix.
- **A claim was written before it was checked** - "selecting no key moves
  nothing" on the lead sheets. Checked afterwards: 249 voicings change, all of
  them spelling only. The wording was corrected; the lesson is the one already
  in `CLAUDE.md` about this file not being evidence.
